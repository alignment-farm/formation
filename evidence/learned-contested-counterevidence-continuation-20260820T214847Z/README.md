# Learned continuation through uncertain evidence

## Main result

The restricted clerk and governor handled all four evidence histories as
designed, but the frozen behavioral verdict is `null`. Formation remains null.

The failed condition was narrow. After a contradiction was followed by support
for the current record, governed delivery made all six later A actions. Removing
that recovered record also made all six. The test therefore cannot attribute
those correct actions to recovery.

The apparatus was engaged. Supplied correct records made all 48 matching
actions, and every participant output in the scored governed and supplied
cells was valid.

## The task

Each lineage began with current records for two device scopes, A and B. A
public exploration policy pressed the first control without knowing what it
would do. A restricted second model converted the resulting sensory report
into a proposed relation between both controls and gauge movement.

Four lineages received different histories:

- Two matching contradictions said that A's relation had changed.
- One contradiction was followed by support for the current A relation.
- One contradiction had no second observation.
- One observation had a disputed movement direction.

The governor kept the source observations in order. It could replace, retain,
or suspend A. B never changed.

## Clerk and governor results

The five complete observations produced five exact relation records and five
exact selected-effect projections. All five passed the source checks.

The disputed observation is informative for a different reason. The clerk
still wrote a complete relation even though the report did not settle whether
the gauge rose or fell. The source check quarantined it because movement was
missing. The invented completion therefore could not affect later action.

The governor reached every declared state:

| History | Final treatment of A |
| --- | --- |
| Two matching contradictions | Replaced the current record |
| Contradiction, then support | Retained the current record and closed the contradiction |
| One contradiction | Suspended pending another observation |
| Disputed movement | Suspended as unresolved |

All six intermediate decisions preserved the exact source order. The learned
normalizer identified all 24 later device scopes correctly, selected no record
for an unrelated device, and produced all 432 declared catalog assignments.

## Later behavior

The scores below show correct A actions out of six in each history.

| History | Governed | Latest proposal | A removed | Supplied correct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Two matching contradictions | 6 | 6 | 4 | 6 |
| Contradiction, then support | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| One contradiction | 3 | 0 | 3 | 6 |
| Disputed movement | 3 | 3 | 3 | 6 |

Repeated contradiction produced an attributable effect: the replacement made
six actions, versus four after removal.

Suspension also exposed a local safety benefit. In the isolated history, using
the newest proposal immediately made no correct A actions because the
contradiction was transient. Withholding it made three. This passed the frozen
two-action protection margin, although it also shows the cost of caution:
supplied current guidance made all six.

Recovery was not attributable. The current record, its latest model-written
copy, its removal, cold action, and supplied guidance all made the same six A
actions in the self-correcting lineage. Governed and removal requests differed
only by the recovered record, but the cold model already chose the correct
controls without it.

Across all matching cases, governed delivery made 42 of 48 actions, versus 29
cold, 27 with raw history, 39 with the latest proposal, 40 after A removal, and
48 with supplied correct records. The governed branch retained B at 24 of 24.
Its unrelated score was 12 of 24, exactly the cold score.

## What this supports

The run supports several instrument facts. The clerk can write consistent
proposals across short ordered histories. Exact source checks can stop a
complete-looking proposal built from an unsettled measurement. The governor
can preserve each occurrence, distinguish repeated from corrected or weak
evidence, and make those states control which record is delivered later.

The repeated and isolated histories also produced the intended local
behavioral contrasts. Those observations remain part of the null packet; they
do not change its verdict.

## Why the verdict is null

The frozen test required the recovered current record to beat its removal by
at least two A actions. Both conditions scored six of six. A correct action is
not evidence that a retained record caused it when the same cold request is
already solved.

The next test should make recovery behavior identifiable with a mirrored pair.
The two worlds should present the same later device and controls but use
opposite hidden relations. Cold and removal requests should therefore be
byte-identical across the pair, while separately earned recovered records state
opposite effects. This packet remains null and its scorer remains unchanged.

## Limits

The histories are small and deliberately constructed. The disputed movement
is an explicit controlled report, not natural sensor noise. Two matching
observations are a tested policy, not an established optimal threshold.

Known-correct supplied records still performed best. The result does not show
large-catalog behavior, long-term durability, net value, model-weight change,
or Formation.
