# Composed clerical revision engagement successor

## Main result

The fresh successor passed its frozen bounded validation. The verdict is
`supported`. Formation remains null.

The composed mechanism admitted every source-supported old and revised record,
quarantined every stale or movement-hidden proposal, and made 45 of 48 matching
actions after revision. Known-correct supplied revisions also made 45 of 48.
Removing the admitted revisions left the old versions current and made none of
the 48 matching actions.

All participant outputs were valid. The engagement correction therefore did
not excuse an invalid output in this run. It prevented a weak control from
having authority to veto the tested interface, while leaving every control
score and denominator unchanged.

## What happened

Four new lineages each began with two devices whose controls had opposite
effects. A 14B participant acted on each source device. A restricted 4B clerk
received the visible design, selected actuator, and gauge movement. It wrote
an old record for each device.

The old records then guided 48 of 48 actions on fresh controls. The environment
reversed all eight relations. Acting from the old records produced eight
movements that contradicted them.

The clerk wrote exposed and movement-hidden revision proposals. Another clerk
call copied the proposed effect for the actuator named in the sensory
transcription. The runtime checked the exact source, exact transcription,
record shape, copied field, and agreement with the observed effect. It did not
infer the effect or choose an action.

Every proposal remained in the lineage. Supported revisions could become the
current version. Rejected proposals stayed quarantined, so their old version
remained current.

Later cases used new device identities, controls, positions, and targets.
Matching cases shared both model-written visible features. Unrelated cases were
novel or recombined one feature from each known design.

The admitted branch made 21 of 24 actions for design A and 24 of 24 for design
B. It also made 21 of 24 downward actions and 24 of 24 upward actions. These are
the frozen subgroup floors.

The three misses all occurred in one A-down cell. The supplied correct revision
made the same three misses on the same request. The learned record therefore
tied the supplied interface rather than introducing a different failure.

The admitted branch exceeded cold by 18 actions, raw history by 21, and stale,
hidden, and removal by 45. Its removal contrast attributes the benefit to the
current admitted revision rather than to hidden continuity in the participant.

## Unrelated behavior

The admitted branch made 16 of 24 unrelated actions, versus 17 cold. The one-
action loss is inside the frozen allowance of three. Its structural matcher
selected no record for any novel or recombined device.

The unrelated actions are cold-model behavior because those requests contained
no selected revision. Their scores can still vary across repeated model calls.
The evidence supports silence by the matcher, not perfect unrelated action by
the participant.

## What this supports

This run supports a bounded two-model substrate for revising retained control
records after counterevidence. A restricted clerk represented what happened.
A deterministic gate admitted only claims tied to complete sensory sources.
The newest admitted version then changed later action on fresh controls and
devices. Stale guidance, hidden-source claims, and removal all lost the benefit.

Together with the earlier learned-instrument validation, the result shows
acquisition, structural selection, one-step revision, causal removal, and
bounded non-transfer in this small domain. The participant did not develop on
its own. The clerk and deterministic governance are parts of the substrate
being tested.

## What this does not support

The learned revision tied known-correct supplied guidance. The test therefore
does not show superiority over static instruction. Its visible categories and
exact post-normalization match were deliberately small. This remains
structured retrieval over model-written fields.

The experiment does not establish repeated revision over a long trajectory,
composition across many records, resistance to interference, net value, or
exceptional practice. It does not establish Formation.

The next empirical problem is longer lineage. The supported mechanism can now
be tested while several admitted records coexist and only one is revised again.
That test should ask whether chronology changes the intended scope without
corrupting the other retained scopes.
