# Mirrored recovery influence

## Main result

The mirrored recovery comparison passed its frozen validation. The verdict is
`supported`. Formation remains null, and the preceding uncertain-evidence run
remains null.

Two worlds presented exactly the same later devices and controls but required
opposite actions. Each world first acquired its own current relation, then saw
one transient contradiction, then saw its current relation again. The governor
suspended after the contradiction and restored the current record after the
confirming observation.

Recovered records made all 48 matching actions. Removing them made 24. Cold
action also made 24, while supplied correct records made all 48.

## Why the mirror matters

The earlier recovery comparison could not identify influence because the cold
model already solved that lineage. This successor removed that accident.

Every later public request was paired across two hidden worlds. The device
description, identifiers, controls, position, target, and allowed actions were
the same. In one world the first control increased position. In the other it
decreased position.

The cold and removal requests were byte-identical across both worlds in all 36
case-and-repeat groups. Those requests could not reveal which relation applied.
Their 24/48 score is the expected mirror split: the same unaided action policy
worked in one world and failed in the other.

Recovered and supplied requests were byte-identical within each world in all
72 pairs. The relation sentence was their only cross-world difference. Both
conditions made every matching action.

## Source and recovery results

The public exploration policy pressed the first control in six source
occurrences. The two worlds produced opposite current records:

- World one recorded that the first control increases position.
- World two recorded that the first control decreases position.

Each world then produced one exact opposite record during a transient reversal
and one exact current record when the relation returned. The restricted clerk
wrote all six records and all six selected-effect projections exactly. The
source checks admitted all six.

In both worlds, the governor first suspended the current record. It then
restored that record and closed the transient contradiction as
uncorroborated. Every decision retained the exact source order.

The recovered record made 24/24 actions in each world and 12/12 in every world
and target direction. Removal returned each world to its unaided directional
bias. The two opposite biases balance to 24/48 only because the public request
contains no hidden-relation information.

The clerk normalized all 12 later descriptions exactly. The runtime made all
288 declared record assignments and selected no record for any unrelated
device.

All unrelated requests were byte-identical across every world and condition.
Every branch scored 0/24. This shows that recovery introduced no unrelated
change. It does not show competent unrelated behavior.

## What this supports

This supports a bounded behavioral contribution from records restored after
self-correction. The participant could not distinguish the mirrored worlds
from their public action requests. Separately earned recovered records supplied
the missing relation, their removal erased the advantage, and supplied copies
produced the same result.

The result also supports the full small mechanism used here: restricted
clerical authorship from external consequences, source-grounded admission,
ordered suspension and recovery, exact structural delivery, and later causal
influence.

## Limits and next question

The worlds were tiny and deterministic except for one authored transient
reversal. The transient was not natural noise, and the two-observation recovery
policy was selected by the experiment rather than learned or shown optimal.

Supplied correct records tied recovered records. Exact scope matching remains a
retrieval operation. The result does not establish a large catalog, long-term
durability, favorable cost, natural-noise robustness, weight change, or
Formation.

The next empirical problem is the tradeoff created by uncertain consequences.
A deterministic specimen should expose how this two-consecutive-observation
policy behaves when a stable world emits isolated anomalies, when a relation
really changes, and when evidence alternates or remains contested. That policy
comparison must preserve every occurrence and keep hidden truth in the scorer,
not the governor.
