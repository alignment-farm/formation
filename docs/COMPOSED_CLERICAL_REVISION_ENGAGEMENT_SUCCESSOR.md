Status: **frozen before contact under the session-wide human authorization**.

## Question

Can the composed revision mechanism pass on four fresh lineages when an invalid
raw-control response remains wrong in its denominator but does not prevent the
tested mechanism from being scored?

The preceding validation observed every mechanism requirement at its frozen
floor. Its formal verdict was `not_engaged` because one raw-history control cell
returned an old action token on all three repeats. The model could express the
supplied and composed-admission comparisons. The raw control could not express
one current-device action. Treating that control failure as evidence that the
tested interface was absent made engagement depend on the wrong branch.

This successor changes only that rule. It does not reinterpret or promote the
earlier verdict.

## Fresh trajectory and comparisons

Four new lineages repeat the complete validation. Each has two designs with
opposite control relations. The clerk writes old records from visible
occurrences. Those records guide pre-change action. The environment then
reverses all eight relations, and old-record action produces visible
contradictory consequences.

The clerk transcribes exposed and movement-hidden sources, writes effect prose,
parses that prose into named fields, and projects the proposed effect for the
observed actuator. The runtime applies the same source, transcription, record,
projection, and equality checks. Supported old and revised versions may be
admitted. Stale and hidden proposals should be quarantined.

After reversal, each lineage again receives A above, A below, B above, B below,
a novel design, and a recombination. Each case receives three calls under the
same eight conditions:

- cold;
- raw counterexperience;
- newest revision after composed admission;
- a stale proposal subjected to the same admission checks;
- a movement-hidden proposal subjected to admission;
- the admitted revision removed, leaving the old version current;
- a supplied correct revision selected by normalized visible scope; and
- the clerk revision selected by environment-owned exact scope.

All calls and all invalid outputs remain in their original fixed denominators.

## Frozen verdict

The apparatus is engaged only if supplied guidance makes at least 43 of 48
matching actions, at least 21 of 24 in each design and target direction, and
every supplied-guidance and composed-admission three-call cell contains at
least two valid actions.

Invalid or unavailable output in any other branch remains invalid and wrong.
It remains visible in the fixed score and evidence. It cannot veto engagement.

Every other frozen requirement is unchanged:

- at least seven of eight old records are exact and make at least 43 of 48
  pre-change actions;
- all eight counteractions follow the old policy into a contradictory result;
- at least seven of eight exposed revised records and selected-effect
  projections are exact;
- all eight old records and all eight exposed revisions are admitted;
- all eight stale and all eight movement-hidden proposals are quarantined;
- at least 20 of 24 later normalizations and selections are exact, with no more
  than two false novel or recombined selections;
- admitted revisions make at least 43 of 48 matching actions and at least 21
  of 24 in each design and target direction;
- admitted revisions exceed cold, raw, stale, hidden, and removal by at least
  16 matching actions each;
- admitted revisions trail supplied guidance by no more than four actions;
- environment-selected clerk revisions make at least 43 matching actions; and
- admitted revisions lose no more than three unrelated actions relative to
  cold.

If engaged admitted revisions lose at least six unrelated actions, the verdict
is `harmful`. Otherwise it is `supported` or `null`. A miss ends this scorer
route; it does not license another scoring change.

This test can support the bounded composed revision mechanism. It cannot
establish Formation, superiority over supplied guidance, long-horizon
development, or practical value.
