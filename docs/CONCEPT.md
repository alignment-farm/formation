# Concept: development without weight updates

Status: **working concept; no mechanism or effect claimed**.

## Object of study

Formation studies a persistent practitioner whose inference engine is a cold,
frozen model. The practitioner can change over time even though the model's
weights do not.

The object of study is the governed causal path:

```text
experience
  -> externally observed consequence
  -> attributed candidate change
  -> governed eligibility decision
  -> eligible disposition, skill, check, or working knowledge
  -> selective influence on later action
  -> continued confirmation, revision, suspension, or revocation
```

The eligibility decision may include a bounded trial or impose probationary
limits.

Every arrow is questionable. A coherent architecture is not evidence that any
arrow works.

The distinctions between occurrence, interpretation, permission to influence,
and attributable influence are load-bearing. A separate pre-admission trial is
not. Trial, probationary use, direct consequence-warranted admission, and other
governance paths are policy choices to compare rather than a universal
formation sequence.

## Experience

An experience is not merely a transcript segment. It is an encounter containing
at least:

- the situation as available before action;
- the practitioner's decision and action;
- the externally observed consequence;
- enough lineage to distinguish observation from later interpretation; and
- the boundary of what was and was not observable at each point.

Some experiences are ambiguous. The system must be allowed to retain them
without forcing a lesson.

An experience does not own the scope in which its consequence should
generalize. That scope is a later interpretation and may be wrong.

## Formation

Formation is a durable change in the practitioner's future preparedness that is
causally attributable to experience and governed across its lifecycle.

Candidate forms include:

- a disposition to notice a structure;
- a check performed before a class of commitments;
- a reusable skill or procedure;
- working knowledge about a particular world or collaborator;
- a changed prior over which action deserves examination; or
- a boundary saying where an otherwise useful pattern must not transfer.

This list is provisional. The project should discover which representations are
actually required rather than build an ontology from names alone.

## Distinctions that must survive

### Formation is not retrieval

Retrieval makes earlier material available. Formation changes how a later
situation is perceived, checked, or acted upon. Retrieval may be one instrument,
but a matching episode is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of formed
competence.

### Formation is not answer copying

A later action that repeats an earlier successful action has weak evidential
value. Transfer cases should change surface form, domain particulars, and action
labels while preserving only the relevant structure.

### Formation is not self-instruction

A model can write a plausible lesson after any outcome. Its proposal is a
candidate, not an earned change. Permission to influence practice depends on
consequence lineage and declared governance. Benefit remains a prospective
experimental verdict.

### Formation is not fine-tuning

Weights remain fixed within the project boundary. This constraint is not a
claim that external formation is superior to training; it makes the persistent
instruments observable, governable, and experimentally separable.

### Formation is not personalization alone

Remembering a user's preference is useful situated knowledge. Formation also
asks whether the practitioner can acquire transferable craft: better judgment,
checks, strategies, and sensitivity to consequential structure.

## The practitioner

“Agent” often names a loop that calls a model and tools. “Practitioner” names the
historically formed system that performs a practice. It includes the model but
is not identical to it.

A practitioner is exceptional only relative to explicit comparators and an
actual practice distribution. Excellence should include judgment about when not
to apply a learned pattern, not only success on positive transfer cases.

## Working vocabulary

These terms constrain the first specifications without selecting a formation
mechanism:

- A **cold model** has fixed weights and begins each scored invocation without
  provider conversation state, hidden recurrence, or an unrecorded prefix. It
  may receive a runtime-created offer. Cold describes the inference component,
  not the practitioner around it.
- A **practitioner state** is the replayable persistent state that may affect
  future practice. It excludes harness assignments, expected answers, and
  hidden evaluation metadata.
- An **encounter** is one bounded situation, decision, action, and observed
  consequence sequence.
- An **experience** is an encounter preserved with provenance and observation
  boundaries. Preservation alone does not imply a lesson.
- A **candidate change** is a versioned interpretation proposing how an
  experience should alter later preparedness, including claimed applicability
  and conditions that would count against it.
- An **admitted change** is a candidate the runtime's declared governance policy
  permits to influence practice. Admission is a policy fact, not evidence that
  the change is beneficial or that the model learned.
- An **activation** is an attributable use of an admitted change at a later
  decision boundary. Availability without influence is not activation.
- A **manifested change** is a measured behavioral difference caused by an
  activation or by the availability of an admitted change.
- **Transfer** is beneficial manifested change on a prospective encounter that
  shares the claimed structure but does not permit answer copying or simple
  episode matching.
- **Negative transfer** is harm caused by a change on encounters where it is
  irrelevant, mis-scoped, contradicted, or no longer warranted.
- **Formation** is established only when experience causes a durable,
  governed change that survives prospective transfer, selectivity, and causal
  tests. A stored candidate or admitted change is formation machinery, not yet
  a formation finding.

## Two-loop architecture

The practice loop performs current work. The formation loop evaluates whether
and how experience should change later work. They interact but have different
timescales and authorities.

The formation loop may observe practice, but it should not rewrite history. The
practice loop may receive an admitted change, but it should not get unrestricted
access to every developmental artifact. Both loops need explicit boundaries so
that causal claims remain testable.

The loops do not imply two models or two continuously running processes. They
name two authorities and causal roles. One implementation may invoke the same
cold model for practice and interpretation, provided the records, inputs, and
permissions remain separable.

## Research questions

1. What minimal event makes an encounter an experience rather than retained
   context?
2. What representation can transfer across surface changes without becoming an
   overgeneral rule?
3. How can consequences license changes without requiring a perfect external
   teacher?
4. Which changes should be active dispositions, callable skills, checks,
   knowledge, or something not yet named?
5. When should governance require a sandboxed trial, allow probationary use, or
   refuse to expose ordinary work to a candidate at all?
6. How are negative transfer, contradiction, drift, and expiry detected?
7. Can formation reduce future context and deliberation cost as competence
   increases?
8. What must remain outside the practitioner so the trajectory harness can
   measure it honestly?

## Initial falsification pressure

The program becomes uninteresting if ordinary retrieval plus strong static
instructions matches every proposed mechanism. It becomes invalid if the
trajectory harness supplies the generalization being credited to the runtime.
It becomes unsafe if positive transfer rises by accepting uncontrolled negative
transfer or by making acquired changes difficult to inspect and revoke.

The first experiments should therefore be designed to let simpler persistence
win.
