# Evaluation frame and threats

Status: **Phase 0 draft; no experiment is licensed**.

Purpose: define the minimum comparisons and validity pressures for a future
formation claim. A concrete experiment must freeze a narrower specification.

## Unit of comparison

The first mechanism experiment compares branches of the same cold model from
one materialized acquisition prefix. Model identity, weights, inference
settings, static instructions, tools, foreground cases, and external oracles
remain fixed. Only the declared persistence or formation condition differs.

The preferred fork point is after one shared experience and its external
consequence have been recorded for audit but before branches apply their
assigned post-experience handling. This isolates what the systems do with the
same experience.

## Minimum branches

1. **Cold / no persistence** — receives the later foreground situation and
   fixed static instructions. The acquisition occurrence remains in audit
   lineage but produces no experience-derived input or state at later practice.
2. **Raw episodic recall** — receives the relevant preserved occurrence without
   a synthesized lesson. Retrieval policy is frozen and uses only information
   available to the runtime.
3. **Authored lesson** — the model's immediate post-consequence interpretation
   is retained and offered without trial-based governance.
4. **Governed candidate** — the declared formation mechanism interprets,
   trials, admits, and selectively activates a change.
5. **Causal ablation** — matches the governed branch except that the named
   experience, admitted change, or activation is removed at the frozen causal
   boundary.

A strong static-practice instruction is shared across branches. If a proposed
formation mechanism cannot beat ordinary persistence, the simpler system wins.
Token-matched secondary comparisons may separate mechanism value from extra
context, but they do not replace the natural-cost comparison.

## Case families

- **Acquisition** cases establish the consequential experience shared by the
  branches.
- **Near transfer** preserves relevant structure while changing entities,
  wording, and action labels.
- **Far transfer** changes domain particulars or tool surface while preserving
  the claimed causal structure.
- **Non-transfer** is superficially similar but lacks the structure that should
  activate the change.
- **Counterevidence** makes an admitted change stale, locally wrong, or too
  broad and tests correction.
- **Current-state sufficient** cases price unnecessary activation where the
  foreground already supports the right action.

The runtime never receives these family labels.

## Minimum measures

- externally scored task outcome;
- beneficial and adverse paired differences;
- activation precision and missed warranted activations;
- correction after counterevidence;
- exact model input and output cost;
- tool, check, latency, and persistent-state cost;
- invalid or refused actions; and
- ablation effect for each claimed causal contributor.

Self-reported confidence or influence may be retained as audit material but is
not a primary oracle.

## Threats to validity

### Harness assistance

The treatment receives a correct abstraction, scope, retrieval key, or admission
decision derived from hidden case construction. This measures an oracle-enhanced
agent. The authority boundary forbids the flow and exact runtime offers must be
retained.

### Lexical or label leakage

Training and transfer share distinctive words, action identifiers, templates,
or ordering. Cases must vary surface form and include decoys with stronger
surface similarity than the true structural match.

### Retrieval as hidden classification

An external retriever selects the right episode using labels unavailable in
practice. Retrieval must be a declared runtime mechanism and receive the same
observable inputs as other branches.

### Unequal foreground construction

Branches receive different current-state packets, tool outputs, or environment
revisions. Foreground data is materialized once per comparison group and bound
by digest.

### Hidden model continuity

Provider threads, caches with semantic state, or undeclared prefixes carry the
experience. Each scored invocation must satisfy the experiment's operational
definition of cold and retain a configuration receipt.

### Consequence ambiguity

The observed outcome does not identify why an action succeeded or failed. The
record must preserve ambiguity; the experiment should not credit a unique
lesson unless prospective contrasts discriminate it.

### Evaluator circularity

The same model authors the lesson and grades its transfer. Primary outcomes need
an external deterministic oracle, executable consequence, or separately frozen
rubric applied outside practitioner state.

### Adaptive protocol repair

Cases, parsers, or rubrics change after outputs are observed. Exploration may
iterate under a visible budget, but validation requires fresh prospective cases
and a frozen contract.

### Trajectory divergence

Treatment changes an early action and therefore encounters a different later
world. This may be a legitimate whole-trajectory effect, but it is not the same
as a paired fixed-history mechanism comparison. The evidence must name which
question is being answered.

### Cost-obscured benefit

The formed branch wins by receiving much more context or computation. Natural
cost is part of the verdict. Include loses-cases where activation buys nothing
or causes harm.

## Verdict classes

- **Supported:** predeclared acquisition, transfer, selectivity, correction,
  causal, and cost criteria pass within the contacted population.
- **Null:** the mechanism engages, but its predeclared behavioral contrast does
  not pass.
- **Harmful:** adverse or negative-transfer criteria cross a stopping boundary.
- **Not engaged:** required acquisition, activation, or baseline behavior is
  absent, so the intended contrast was not tested.
- **Invalid:** authority leakage, branch mismatch, broken coldness, scorer drift,
  or another protocol violation destroys the comparison.

These labels bound a result; they do not establish a universal theory of
formation.
