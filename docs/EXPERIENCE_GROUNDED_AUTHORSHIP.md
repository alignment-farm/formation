# Experience-grounded authorship problem

Status: **independently review-stable analytical boundary after the completed
phase-coupled contact; no mechanism, successor protocol, cases, budget,
implementation, or model call licensed**.

## Main point

Formation has reached a problem earlier than candidate influence. The cold
model must first turn an occurrence and its consequence into a candidate that
can apply beyond the episode. In the completed phase-coupled contact, both
model-authored `change` fields were exact control tokens from the acquisition
device. Those strings had no meaning on later devices, so the frozen governor
refused both candidates.

The result must not become another admission screen. The model participated,
acted, observed consequence, and produced awkward interpretations. That is the
developmental behavior to study.

The next question is:

> How can a consequential occurrence support a reusable, scope-bounded
> relation without the runtime, schema, governor, scorer, or static instruction
> writing that relation for the practitioner?

This is narrower than a Formation claim. A later candidate may remain absent,
wrong, brittle, unused, or indistinguishable from ordinary prompt content.

## Evidence that created the problem

The [phase-coupled contact](PHASE_COUPLED_EXPLORATORY_CHARTER.md) completed all
69 planned calls with no retry or action repair. Its
[evidence record](../evidence/phase-coupled-exploratory-contact-20260818/README.md)
shows:

- both two-action acquisitions were observable and identified their hidden
  family profiles;
- both interpreter responses were parseable two-field objects;
- World A copied one acquisition control into each field;
- World B copied one acquisition control into `change` and used the occurrence
  field name `movement_direction` as `counterevidence`;
- the pre-frozen governor refused both candidates without semantic repair; and
- all governed and ablation deliveries were consequently null, so no
  authorized candidate-content comparison existed.

The contact does not show that Qwen cannot author a reusable relation. It shows
only what happened under one occurrence representation and one one-shot
authorship responsibility. It also does not show that a more abstract candidate
would be correct, useful, or causally influential.

## The responsibility that remains at risk

The practitioner, not the apparatus, must still do all of the following:

1. distinguish episode identifiers from reusable structure;
2. state what changed in preparedness because of the observed consequence;
3. limit that change to a warranted scope;
4. name later evidence that should weaken or overturn it;
5. distinguish empirical counterevidence from a governor's policy rejection;
6. revise or withdraw a candidate when a declared process warrants doing so;
   and
7. use an admitted candidate in later action when it applies.

These are separable events. Producing admissible text does not establish useful
content. Admission does not establish use. A later action difference does not
establish acquired competence.

## What is not yet known

At least four explanations remain live:

- **Occurrence pressure.** Opaque controls dominated the occurrence and made
  token extraction more salient than the phase-to-slot relation.
- **One-shot compression.** A single candidate call gave the practitioner no
  opportunity to inspect, challenge, or revise its first interpretation.
- **Responsibility ambiguity.** The generic `change` and `counterevidence`
  responsibility may not have made the intended developmental act legible,
  even though its output shape was clear.
- **Inference limit.** The exact model may not reliably infer the reusable
  relation from that experience under the frozen public rule.

The completed record does not choose among them. A successor that changes
several at once would not reveal which pressure mattered.

## Apparent fixes that do not solve the problem

Do not count any of the following as experience-grounded authorship:

- a schema with phase, slot, increase, decrease, or inverse fields that names
  the relation to be discovered;
- a runtime extractor that converts action and consequence into the correct
  family profile;
- a governor that rewrites a copied token as a slot relation;
- repeated sampling until an oracle finds acceptable wording;
- returning the already-present rule against episode-token reuse as an error
  message and calling compliance with that message experience-grounded;
- a stronger prompt whose examples contain the desired relation;
- a later deterministic applicator that performs the conditional reasoning; or
- selecting a different model because its first candidate is easier to admit.

Shape constraints may remain identical instrumentation across branches. They
may not be treated as evidence of authorship or development.

## Authority and causal order

The completed candidate and any future candidate are model-authored. The
runtime records them. The governor may accept or reject them under a frozen
policy. A policy rejection is not an environment consequence and is not
empirical counterevidence to the candidate's meaning.

If a future mechanism asks the model to author again after rejection, the
record must show the actual causal sequence:

```text
candidate proposed v1 by model
  -> candidate rejected by governor with policy basis
  -> second model invocation with exact retained request parents
  -> candidate proposed v2, candidate withdrawn, or observable no proposal
```

Version 2 remains model-authored, but its request has runtime and governor
causal parents. Those parents must be retained. The event is not a new practice
encounter unless a new environment occurrence actually happens.

The occurrence projection, including field names such as
`movement_direction`, is part of the occurrence. Changing that projection is an
occurrence-representation intervention. It must not be combined silently with
an authoring-process intervention.

## Analytical routes still open

No next mechanism is selected. At least three routes require separate causal
accounts:

1. **More or different experience.** A second environment consequence or a
   model-chosen probe may supply developmental evidence that one acquisition
   did not. This changes the occurrence history, not merely authorship wording.
2. **More interpretive process.** The practitioner may inspect or revise its
   own first candidate without receiving a policy lesson. This changes call
   structure and draft availability while holding occurrence content fixed.
3. **Governor feedback.** The practitioner may receive an exact rejection
   basis. In this contact, `episode_control_token_reuse` would mostly restate a
   rule already present in the first request. A resulting revision would first
   be evidence of response to policy feedback, not experience-grounded
   authorship.

A fourth live result is that none of these interactions yields a reusable
candidate with the exact model and domain. That would be contact evidence, not
an admission failure.

## Comparator pressure for a revision hypothesis

If the feedback route remains under consideration, it must first survive a
matched-call analysis. The minimum conceptual branches are:

- a second independent authorship call with the same occurrence but no first
  draft and no rejection;
- a second call with the exact first draft but no rejection basis;
- a second call with the exact first draft and exact policy-coded rejection
  basis; and
- a static-instruction call whose generic authorship guidance contains neither
  the realized family profile nor an example of the missing relation.

These are not frozen branches. They expose the confounds that a proposal must
resolve: another sample, another call, draft replay, policy highlighting, and
static instruction. The completed first call is not a matched second-call
control.

The revision request must declare whether it repeats the occurrence, public
rule, first candidate, and rejection receipt. Any runtime prose surrounding
those objects is part of the intervention. Harness-only family, profile,
expected-action, branch, score, and verdict information remains forbidden.

The completed governor predicate stays fixed for this analysis. Redesigning
admission after seeing the refusals is a different problem. A rejection receipt
may use a frozen policy code, but neither the code nor its rendering may contain
the realized phase binding, slot answer, inverse, later action, prospective
control, or occurrence-specific coaching.

Even with these controls, a feedback-associated candidate difference would not
show that experience produced the candidate. If the feedback branch merely
matches the static-instruction branch, the useful description is
`instruction-equivalent revision`, not development.

## Authorship-only boundary

The next analytical object ends at candidate authorship. It does not require an
admitted candidate or later action to count as contact. Candidate availability,
versioning, withdrawal, rejection, and null output are the primary observables.
Later influence remains a separate problem that may be revisited only after an
authorship mechanism exists; it cannot serve as the success condition for
choosing one.

No candidate interaction described here licenses a charter. A separate
mechanism proposal must first freeze one causal route and its comparators. A
later charter would then need its own independent stability review, strict
budget, stopping rule, and fake-tested runner review before any model call.

## Loses-conditions

An authorship mechanism is rejected before a charter if:

- it succeeds when the model merely follows a form error or a restatement of
  the public rule;
- it changes extra sampling, call count, draft replay, and feedback together
  while attributing the result to one of them;
- a schema, static instruction, governor message, runtime wrapper, or scorer
  states the realized family binding;
- governor rejection is recorded as empirical counterevidence or a new
  environment occurrence;
- the harness selects or rewrites a favorable candidate;
- interesting measurement occurs only after a repaired admission; or
- refusal triggers model search, repeated resampling, or post hoc prompt repair.

## Analytical completion condition

This problem is ready for a mechanism proposal only when two independent
reviewers have tried and failed to reduce the selected route to extra sampling,
prompt highlighting, draft replay, static instruction, answer leakage, or
admission screening. They must also reconstruct its authority and lineage
events and name a null trajectory that completes without later practice.

That review would license only a separate mechanism proposal. It would not
license cases, a budget, implementation, a charter, a runner, model contact, or
a Formation finding.

## Review record

Composer 2.5 and Grok 4.6 first returned `REVISE_PROBLEM`. Both showed that the
draft's preferred one-revision loop was a screen-and-retry design: the governor
would mostly restate the no-token-reuse rule already present in the first
request, and interesting behavior would remain gated on repaired admission.
They also found unmatched call-count, draft-replay, policy-feedback, and static
instruction confounds.

The repaired problem selects no route, separates policy rejection from
empirical consequence, makes lineage parents explicit, freezes the completed
governor for analysis, ends at authorship, and states the comparator pressure
that any feedback proposal would have to survive. The same two read-only Cursor
reviewers then returned:

- `composer-2.5`: `PROBLEM_STABLE`
- `cursor-grok-4.6-high-fast`: `PROBLEM_STABLE`

These verdicts stabilize the analytical problem only. They do not select or
license a mechanism proposal or contact.
