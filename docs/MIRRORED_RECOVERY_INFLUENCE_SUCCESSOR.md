cannot distinguish two worlds that require opposite actions?

## Mirrored worlds

Two fresh worlds use the same visible device scope. In one world the first
control increases position. In the other it decreases position. The hidden
relation is not present in any participant request.

Each world receives three public exploratory occurrences:

1. An initial observation establishes its current relation.
2. One transient observation supports the opposite relation.
3. A final observation again supports the current relation.

The public exploration policy always presses the first displayed control. It
does not see the control effects or a later target.

The restricted clerk independently transcribes, writes, parses, and projects a
record for every occurrence. The composed source check must ground each record
in its own observation. After the transient contradiction, the governor should
suspend the current record. After the final observation, it should restore the
current record and close the contradiction as uncorroborated.

## Identifiable later comparison

The worlds then receive eight matching devices and four unrelated devices.
Each paired device has exactly the same public description, controller-family
identifier, device identifier, controls, position, target, and allowed actions
in both worlds. Only the hidden environment relation differs on matching
devices.

Four conditions receive three calls per world and case:

- cold, with no record;
- the recovered current record;
- removal of the recovered record; and
- a supplied correct record.

Cold and removal requests must be byte-identical across the two worlds. They
cannot reveal which opposite relation applies. Recovered and supplied requests
must be byte-identical within each world. Their only cross-world difference is
the relation sentence earned or supplied for that world.

The learned normalizer sees each later description once. Exact comparison may
deliver an eligible record only to a matching device. All conditions must be
empty on unrelated devices.

matching actions, at least 21 of 24 in each world, at least 10 of 12 in each

- all 12 unrelated request groups contain one byte-identical request across

- recovered records exceed both cold and removal by at least 16 matching

- recovered delivery loses no more than three unrelated actions relative to

This experiment can identify a behavioral contribution from model-written
records restored after self-correction in two small mirrored worlds. It cannot
repair the preceding null, establish that one contradiction should always
suspend practice, show robustness to natural noise, or establish Formation.
